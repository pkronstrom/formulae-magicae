# Ground work for a light timber building in Finland — practice, not design

What a ground-work phase can cite. General practice for a light (< 30 m²) building on
piers; it is not a geotechnical design. Soil type, slope and water change everything —
say so on the phase page.

- **Routa.** Ground frost reaches 1–2.5 m depending on region and winter. A light hut does
  not go below it; it is built to tolerate movement (piers on a compacted non-frost-
  susceptible bed) or to reduce it (frost insulation around the footprint).
- **Bed.** Strip turf and topsoil ~0.5 m beyond the footprint, shape the ground to fall
  away from the centre. Suodatinkangas (geotextile), then 200–300 mm murske 0–32 in two
  lifts, each compacted (rented tärylevy, half a day).
- **Setting out.** String lines from batter boards; check diagonals equal before anything
  is cast. This is the only place millimetre accuracy on the ground matters.
- **Footings and piers.** Anturat (e.g. 400 × 400 × 100 mm kuivabetoni) levelled to one
  plane with a laser; then pilariharkko (Leca P-240, 240 × 240 × 195 mm) courses on mortar,
  cores filled, an anchor (kierretanko M12 or kulmarauta) set in the corner piers for the
  rim. Alternative: adjustable screw piers (ruuvipaalu) where the ground allows.
- **Frost insulation (optional).** EPS/XPS ≥ 100 mm laid ~1 m out around the footprint,
  50 mm murske over it, keeps the ground under the piers from freezing.
- **Crawl space.** Keep it ventilated and the ground under it dry; a moisture barrier on
  the ground is common under a closed floor.
- **Curing.** Mortar and concrete a week before loading.
- **Permit.** ≤ 30 m² vapaa-ajan rakennus is often toimenpideilmoitus or exempt, but it
  depends on the kunta — the phase page says "ask the kunta", nothing more.

Vocabulary: routa (ground frost), murske (crushed rock), suodatinkangas (geotextile),
antura (footing), pilariperustus (pier foundation), tärylevy (plate compactor),
routaeriste (frost insulation), ryömintätila (crawl space).
