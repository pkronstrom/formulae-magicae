# Manufacturer cost estimate

Run after architecture, in parallel with feasibility, while parts and assembly choices can still change. Use it for delivery projects by default and on request for design or package work. An indicative price can inform a design decision before Gerbers exist. The release guide covers the later file-based quote.

**Minimum inputs.** Use the brief and provisional BOM. Record the requested number of usable gadgets, destination and comparison currency, acceptable work on arrival, and budget if given. Define a common build scenario with estimated PCB dimensions, layer count, thickness, finish, assembly sides and any special processes. Include enclosure material and quantity, programming, tests and final assembly when requested. Label provisional assumptions so each supplier comparison can be repeated.

**Compare suppliers.** Start with at least two plausible routes, usually JLCPCB, PCBWay or Seeed Fusion for PCBA. Use the [delivery guide](phase-delivery.md) for service links. A route can combine a PCB assembler, enclosure supplier and home assembly. If a provider can only deliver a populated board and the user wants a finished gadget, show who does the remaining work and what it costs. If the user mandates one supplier, respect that choice and show variants there. If only one comparable estimate is obtainable, keep the other route visible with the missing evidence.

Use current public configurators, dated supplier price schedules or existing project quotes. Record the URL or quote identifier, date, settings and validity when available. Label each figure as configured estimate, published rate, supplier quote or explicit assumption. Marketing starting prices alone cannot establish the price of a configured build. Use a reasoned range where justified. Mark unavailable figures as unknown. Contact suppliers or upload project files only when the user authorizes sharing them.

**Compute the comparison.** For each route, record bare-board fabrication, assembly setup/stencil and placement, components and sourcing fees, and any enclosure, programming, testing or final assembly in scope. Include shipping for each parcel, applicable taxes or duties and any unpaid work left to the user. Count bundled charges once. Show ordered boards, assembled units and finished units separately where minimum quantities differ. Convert to a common currency with a dated rate where needed. Divide the batch total by the number of usable units delivered at the stated completion level. Show a known subtotal and named omissions wherever a delivered total cannot be supported.

| Supplier route | Delivered scope and quantity | Batch total or known subtotal | Per delivered unit | Main uncertainty or work on arrival |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One row per route | State what arrives | Currency, range and inclusions | Same scope and currency | Identify any cost that could change the choice |

**Capture `pricing`.** Output `estimate` as a tracked comparison with the assumptions, itemized costs and sources, plus `visual` as a labeled comparison table or chart image for the offline board. Show quantity, currency, scope, and unknowns on the visual. The `comparison` check verifies source dates, arithmetic, scope comparability, exclusions and whether the budget can be assessed. Give the reason for the preferred route when evidence supports one. A supplier with a missing shipping or assembly cost cannot win on delivered price from its subtotal alone.

Capture the comparison with `capture pricing --output estimate=pricing/estimate.md --output visual=pricing/comparison.svg`. Use repeated `--uses` flags for separately maintained inputs and saved calculator evidence. Refresh after a material change to quantity, BOM, board process, assembly sides, enclosure or destination. Estimates above a hard ceiling reopen the specific design decision responsible. Record conditional savings against a named change, such as one-sided assembly. A rough estimate adds no purchase authorization or human gate.
